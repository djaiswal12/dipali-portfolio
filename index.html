About
Brand systems, ready for fashion.
Open to brand design & art direction — fashion, apparel, lifestyle

I'm a Toronto-based brand designer and marketing specialist with almost five years of experience helping consumer packaged goods and lifestyle brands show up consistently — across digital, retail, and multi-platform campaigns. Right now, I'm Design Lead & Marketing Specialist at Staedtler.

My work turns marketing strategy into creative people actually respond to. I specialize in integrated product launches, seasonal campaigns, and high-volume asset production — all while keeping brand consistency tight. I work closely with marketing, product, and sales teams so the creative lines up with business goals and retail timelines, and I bring a production background that keeps things efficient, clear, and accurate right down to the file.

CPG and fashion ask for the same discipline: a seasonal calendar, a high volume of assets, retail timelines that don't move, and a brand that has to look like itself everywhere it shows up. My Staedtler and retail packaging work is proof I can run that — I'm looking to bring it to a fashion, apparel, or lifestyle brand next.

Bachelor of Design, OCAD University · Toronto, ON

DIDI Tote Bags
Elevated totes carrying South Asian–inspired art — made by hand, from materials given a second life.
“Didi means sister. It's the name I've always been called — and, fittingly, what my sister calls me back.”
01 — The brand
The brand takes its name from didi — sister, in my mother tongue. It's what I've always been called, and, fittingly, what my own sister calls me in return. The name carries the idea the whole brand is built on: that the things worth keeping are the ones made and passed between hands. Every bag is made that way — by hand, from materials given a second life, carrying artwork drawn from the traditions I grew up with.
DIDI is a small brand of elevated tote bags featuring South Asian–inspired art and handmade jewelry. I designed and made every part of it — the identity, the artwork, the bags, the jewelry, the packaging, the shop, and the photography.

Building a brand alone means every decision is yours and every gap is yours too — there is no one to hand the packaging to, or the photography, or the shipping. Wraps & fleets
Vehicle Graphics
I led vehicle graphics end to end — measuring vehicles on site, working directly with clients on the brief, and preflighting artwork so the design survived the jump to a curved, paneled metal surface. A wrap is unforgiving: what looks right flat can break across a wheel arch or a door seam.
Featured project
The 14-vehicle fleet.
Fourteen wraps across different vehicle types, one design system. The challenge wasn't drawing it once — it was holding it consistent across vehicles with different proportions, panel breaks, and surface geometry, so the fleet reads as one brand parked side by side.
Fleet lineup — the system as it reads across the full set.
One system, four body types
{{ t.label }} — {{ t.note }}
Where the system adapts
{{ d.label }}
Fourteen vehicles meant fourteen measurement sets, fourteen preflight passes, and one install calendar. I sequenced the artwork so production was never waiting on approvals, tracked revisions per vehicle so nothing was applied from a stale file, and kept sales, client, and installers working from the same schedule. All fourteen delivered on time.
